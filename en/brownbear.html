Brown Bear

Japan's largest land animal. It stands at the summit of the northern forest.

Scientific name Ursus arctos yesoensis
English name Brown bear
Class Carnivora, Ursidae
Range Forests of Hokkaido
Length 180–280 cm
Weight Approx. 100–500 kg
Notes Japan's largest land animal. Omnivorous; in autumn it also catches fish migrating up the rivers. It hibernates in a den through the winter.

Ruler of the Forest

With an overwhelming bulk and powerful muscle, the brown bear reigns at the summit of the rich northern forest. In its senses, which react sharply to the rustle of trees and the faintest sound, and in the weighty bearing with which it treads the earth in silence, dwell the severity of the true wild and an overwhelming nobility.

The solid, composed manner it shows in our exhibit area, and the deep gaze that suddenly settles on the distance, quietly convey the unshakable presence that belongs only to a creature that has carried the vast nature of the northern land on its own shoulders.

The Season They Gather at the River

As the seasons turn and the fish come running up the rivers, the brown bears' lives reach a major turning point. Wading boldly into the cold current and seizing prey with power at a moment's opening, they tell of the superb hunting skill and outstanding physical ability they have honed over long years.

Taking in nature's rich bounty with their whole body while deftly adapting to the seasonal changes of their environment, their way of life teaches us just how dynamic and finely balanced the food chain of the northern forest truly is.

Hibernation as a Strategy

Before the arrival of the bitterly cold winter, the brown bear adopts an intricate survival strategy: entrusting its own body to a deep sleep. Storing plenty of nourishment through autumn, the months spent in a den dug deep in the snowy forest are a time for quietly guarding the flame of life.

This astonishing physiological mechanism — awaiting spring's awakening without food or drink — is the crystallization of the evolution they have acquired to survive the harsh northern climate. Their way of living, wholly surrendering to the cycle of the seasons, quietly tells of a life lived together with nature.

Living Side by Side with People

Within Hokkaido's vast nature, the brown bear has long lived as a presence right alongside our own lives. A ruler that symbolizes the richness of the forest, it also poses an essential theme when we consider the many issues surrounding the boundary between people and wild animals.

To face the overwhelming presence of a brown bear at a zoo is not merely to observe a single animal. It becomes a profound opportunity to look anew at the whole picture of the ecosystem the northern land holds, and at how we ourselves should face the natural environment.
