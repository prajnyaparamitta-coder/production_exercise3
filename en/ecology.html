Animal Life

What they eat, how they sleep, how they spend the day. The closer you look, the less it feels like a distant world.

How They Spend the Day

We don't insist on fixed "show times" for our animals. From waking in the morning to falling asleep at night — how they feel the wind, where they laze about, when they leap into the water — much of it is left to the animals' own mood and daily rhythm.

Rather than performing on cue inside a tidy cage, they wander as they please, or curl up to sleep on a cool rock. What flows here is their real, unscripted "everyday time." Meeting a completely different expression depending on when you visit is part of what makes this zoo special.

About Their Food

We don't simply toss the food in all at once and call it "feeding time." To recreate the way northern animals find food in the wild, we hide their food and let them search, or deliberately make them take time and use their heads.

The intent gaze of a polar bear chasing fish in an icy pool, or the sound of a sika deer deftly crunching tough bark — this is not mere "feeding," but an important moment that gently switches on the wildness they naturally carry. In the dynamic expressions of that instant, you can feel the sheer vitality of northern life.

Seasons and Life

Hokkaido's climate dramatically changes the animals' coats and behavior. When deep-snow winter arrives, the cold-hardy animals grow lively — truly in their element — dashing across the snowfields as round silhouettes wrapped in thick winter fur.

As the seasons turn, the animals' lives change dynamically too. Rather than keeping the same conditions year-round with artificial climate control, we let them live while accepting the north's harsh cold and clear air as they are. It's here, in that, that the "true ecology" this zoo most wants to show can be found.
